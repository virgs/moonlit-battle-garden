# Game specification

Status: design in progress. See decisions.md for agreement status; proposals here must not silently become confirmed requirements.

## Identity and loop

Two rival gardens. Each player hits moles before they retreat, avoids rabbits and empty holes, collects stars, and casts character-specific magic to survive or disrupt the opponent. No stacking mechanic.

Proposed baseline: each garden is a 3×3 board. Misses and incorrect hits reduce health. Depleting health loses the round; first to two round wins wins the match. Spawn pressure increases over time. Score is visible, but its formula and relationship to victory remain undecided.

Proposed magic economy: collectible stars charge a meter capped at four. Casting uses the character's spell at the exact current tier and consumes the charge. Lower tiers should remain useful tactical choices rather than strictly weaker versions of higher tiers.

## Modes and presentation

| Mode | Presentation | Connection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local human versus human | Two boards on one computer | Offline |
| Online human versus human | Proposed two-board desktop layout; own board on mobile | Online |
| Human versus AI | Device-appropriate layout | Offline supported |

Mobile must prioritize large touch targets and the player's board, score, health, stars, and casting control. Proposed opponent strip: portrait, health, stars, and rounds won. Cross-device matches are intended; input fairness needs playtesting and a policy decision.

Matches fill the viewport with one canvas; menus, status, controls, and pause/results UI are HUD overlays. Preserve image proportions with uniform scaling and responsive positioning. Sprite feet and hit areas share measured source-art anchors. On two-board views, the local player is always left, and same-computer Player 1 is always left. No side selector is needed. Future online transport must map server player identity into this local presentation. Single-board mobile views show the local player; phone landscape can place HUDs beside the board to keep targets large.

Original direct keyboard layout is a starting proposal: QWE/ASD/ZXC for one player and numpad 789/456/123 for the other. Casting keys, rebinding, mouse, gamepad, and touch details are not finalized. Keyboard-mapping shuffle is excluded.

## AI

Easy, medium, and hard opponents must run offline. Use ordinary game logic, not an external language model.

Proposed differentiation:

- Easy: slower reactions, more misses and rabbit mistakes, predictable casting.
- Medium: moderate reaction speed and accuracy, basic recovery and star-saving decisions.
- Hard: fast but fallible reactions, target prioritization, strategic spell timing.

Proposed fairness constraints: bots react only after targets become visible, obey a minimum interval between actions, and respect the same spell and hit rules as humans. Do not grant advance spawn knowledge or extra health. Difficulty values require playtesting.

## Technical direction

Frontend: TypeScript, React, Phaser, Vite, pnpm; follow shinobi-arena's tooling conventions, including Vitest and Playwright where appropriate. React handles menus/room screens; Phaser handles board rendering, animation, and effects. No physics requirement has been identified.

Proposed architecture: a shared TypeScript rules simulation independent of rendering. Offline modes run locally. Online matches run the rules on an authoritative server, with clients sending hit/cast actions. Render feedback locally while reconciling authoritative results. Server owns spawns, legal hits, health, spell effects, and outcomes. Timing tolerance must account for network latency without blindly trusting client timestamps.

Recommended online infrastructure: PartyServer with Cloudflare Durable Objects, one room per match. Start with private invite rooms. Backend selection is still open. Networking services must not be required for local or AI matches.

Recommended mobile delivery: responsive browser game with PWA installation and offline caching. Native packaging is a later decision.

## Art and sound

Reuse suitable assets from Molecide Gaiden and Dig Hole Battle Garden. Molecide supplies a character atlas, rise/idle/hit/miss animations, gardens, HUD assets, and sound effects. The older game supplies character portraits and additional effects/art/audio. Inspect assets and their provenance before selecting final release content; avoid assuming every repository asset is original.

PixelLab fills specific asset gaps after the initial character and spell requirements are defined. Preserve the original cast's playful identities and recognizable props.

## Proposed spell readability rules

- Show the currently available spell next to the star meter.
- Give hostile spells a brief recognizable visual/audio warning and clear duration feedback.
- Limit simultaneous disruptive effects so boards remain playable.
- Define automatic clearing explicitly: proposed safe rabbit removal, no mole combo rewards, and stars left for manual collection.
- Avoid long interruptions to play for casting animations.

## Delivery scope

First release focuses on two characters with complete magic kits, local versus, online versus, mobile presentation, and three AI difficulties. Exact release checks and sequencing are still open. Keep the other six character concepts documented for expansion after this version is ready. Local/AI play is now implemented; see prototype.md for actual defaults. Offline AI self-play at all three difficulties is required to evaluate balance. Online and PWA delivery remain pending.
